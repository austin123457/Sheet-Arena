# Sheet Arena

D&D 5e (2024 rules) character sheets for fictional characters, built canon-first, and a dice-driven arena that fights them against each other.

Twenty fighters, one sheet each: Goblin Slayer, Guts, Kyle Crane, Itachi Uchiha, Adam Smasher, Jin Sakai, Might Guy (the canon merge), the Doom Slayer, Link the Hero of Time, Battle Beast, Alexander Anderson, Arthur Morgan, Spike Spiegel, Talion, V, Kratos, Miyamoto Musashi, Dark Link, Handsome Jack, and FLLFFL (Alfa, of the Rock Hard Gladiators). The sheets are PDFs in `sheets/pdf/`; the scripts that render the ones Claude built are in `sheets/build/` (reportlab). Caleb's sheets (Smasher, Jin, Crane v3, Battle Beast, Arthur, Talion v2) and Alfa's are PDFs only.

## Run a fight

    python battle.py 1v1 slayer link -n 400
    python battle.py team slayer+guy smasher+jin
    python battle.py ffa                      # the whole roster
    python battle.py matrix -n 200            # every 1v1
    python battle.py solo link                # Link vs everyone at once
    python battle.py 1v1 v smasher            # V picks his OS; v-sandy / v-deck force it
    python battle.py 1v1 link dark-link-room  # the shadow in its own room, translucent for the first minute
    python battle.py 1v1 link-no-nayru dark-link   # an exhibition rule: Nayru's Love stays in the pack
    python battle.py list

Dice come from the OS (`secrets`), nothing is seeded, and a fight caps at 40 rounds and counts as a draw.

## The rulings the arena uses

- Open sand, daylight, 60 ft apart, everyone fresh off a long rest, no surprise. The walls are out of bounds. A tie on initiative is a coin toss.
- Teams and free-for-alls coordinate on "the wizard first, then the lowest."
- "Ignores Resistance" (Smasher's and Battle Beast's Irresistible Offense) does not pierce immunity. Only Night Guy and the Crucible are written to.
- Guy's Gate of Death and Anderson's Nail are desperation moves (under 70 HP); Crane's Beast Meter starts empty after a rest; the Fierce Deity's Mask only works in a boss's lair, so Link never has it here; Izanami needs the target to have struck Itachi on two separate turns, and then it ends the fight.
- Massive damage (5e's rule): a hit that drops you to 0 with at least your maximum HP left over kills you outright. Relentless Rage, Iron Will, death saves and the Berserker Armor don't answer it; only a way back from death does: Link's fairies, and Kratos's Out of Hades (he was dead every time he climbed out). V's Second Heart restarts a heart, not a body that isn't there, and Spike's Nine Lives is luck, so massive damage beats both; Talion's Banished from Death brings him back at the next nightfall, after the fight. Raid bosses keep their own rules. On the ladder it's almost only Night Guy, which does about 14,000 to a 236-HP Guts.
- Evasion works for everyone whose sheet has it (Jin, Guy, Spike, Alfa): nothing on a made Dex save, half on a failed one, unless they're incapacitated.
- Anderson dies only to a single blow for his full 284 hit points, his heart torn out (see Kratos), or once the Nail is in and breaks. Down, he gets up at 30 at the start of his turn unless 20 radiant or fire landed on him since his last one.
- Kratos is the one fighter who uses the heart clause on Anderson's sheet: once Anderson is down, he grapples him and wins a Strength contest. His Blades of Chaos keep the fire coming so nothing regrows in between.
- Peak versions: living, healthy Itachi (the Edo body can't lose to anyone without a seal); canon Guy with Night Guy at 60d12.
- Itachi fights with his whole slot table (4/3/3/3/2/1/1/1/1) and concentration: genjutsu first (Tsukuyomi on whoever will probably fail it), Susanoo when what is coming would kill him, then Totsuka or Yasaka Magatama inside it, Amaterasu for the one who can't be reached, and once Susanoo is gone Dominate Person, Hold Person, Phantasmal Killer or Phantasmal Force. Shadow Clone every time he has a spare 4th-level slot (the clone opens with Mind Sliver before his save spells, and enemies can't tell which Itachi is real), Mislead when he's low, Mirror Image, Shield and Illusory Self, Hypnotic Pattern and Fireball for groups, Counterspell against 3rd-level spells and up (Anderson's Haste and Hold Monster, Talion's Steel Wind Strike and Conjure Barrage). Each Mangekyo casting costs a level of exhaustion (-2 on his d20 tests; six is death) and Susanoo ending costs two, as his sheet says; he never spends the level that would kill him. Left out: what does nothing on open sand (Misty Step, Disguise Self, Silent Image, Dispel Magic, the rituals) and what is strictly worse than something he has (Scorching Ray and the kunai below Fire Bolt, Tidal Wave below Fireball, Summon Beast and Summon Fey, whose concentration he always has a better use for).
- Battle Beast by his sheet: charm and fear immunity only while raging (no stun immunity), +27 on a natural 20, Hew after a crit or a kill, his Worthy Opponent has disadvantage attacking anyone else, Intimidating Presence (DC 22, a save each turn) on everyone not immune, paid for with Rage uses after the free one, and Glorious Death: one last swing at the worthy opponent who drops him for good.
- Talion has no army here: Brand works only on orcs and beasts, and his sheet says it doesn't matter against one foe. He plays it the sheet's way: mark first, Freeflow Counter every melee blow, bank six Might for Elven Rage, and spend a single Execution only on a foe at 30 HP or fewer.
- Crane's finisher (a claw hit that leaves an ordinary creature at 15 HP or fewer kills it) never fires here: his sheet excludes bosses, and everyone in this arena is one. The same goes for V's Suicide, System Collapse, Cyberpsychosis and Memory Wipe.
- Musashi is the historical man at about fifty, with the feats Yoshikawa's novel and *Vagabond* give him wherever they only make history's number bigger. His guard (+6 AC with both swords in hand) and Holding Down the Pillow (the long sword's Reaction: he cuts into a blow that would land, and if his cut hits, the blow misses) work against melee attacks only, and never against a critical hit. He spends One Cut on his first long-sword hit, with every superiority die but one. At 0 he's down like anyone else.
- Dark Link is the shadow from the Water Temple, and his sheet is a rule more than a stat block: the game gives him two points of health for every heart the hero has, so he's the hero's own sheet at the hero's level (on the ladder, the level-20 Hero of Time: 224 HP, AC 20, four swings, both Action Surges, the dice), with a copy of the sword and the shield and nothing else. No pack, no magic, no fairies, no Second Wind, no Evil's Bane. What he adds is the fight from the game: he mirrors a melee weapon swung in one hand (he rolls a d20, and if it equals or beats the number on your die the blades meet and you miss), staggers on the sixth clank, answers a miss with his Reaction, sinks into the floor under the first blow that hurts him each turn, and rolls away from arrows and bullets. Which weapons are one-handed is a trait on each fighter (`one_handed`): Link, Anderson, Talion, Goblin Slayer, Musashi, Crane's machete, Kratos's Axe, V's Mantis Blades. Kratos and V know it, and take the Blades of Chaos and the two-handed Scalpel against him; Alfa takes the Jet Sword in both hands (1d10+8, and Dueling is gone). That he's a creature of evil (so the real Master Sword bites) and can't be poisoned, charmed or frightened are rulings; no source says what he is. On the ladder he walks in solid. `dark-link-room` is the fight as the game has it, translucent for ten rounds, when he mirrors and evades and never attacks.
- Handsome Jack is the man, not the company: an Artificer (Artillerist) 20 on the 2025 class, 143 HP, Strength 10, and everything he does is something he built. A wrist laser (Fire Bolt through a Wand of the War Mage, 4d10 + 1d8), Scorching Ray on his big slots, two Eldritch Cannons on one bonus action (one turns Protector whenever his shield is down), a cloak (Invisibility in his Spell-Storing Item; he opens under it when the other side moved first, or against Arthur, Musashi and Dark Link, where it tested better), Shield, Lucky, Flash of Genius, and one homebrew feature, Expendable Assets: two Digi-Jacks for the first minute, and a d6 for each when an attack hits him, 4 or better and it hit the hologram. Soul of Artifice is run one item at a time: each time he drops he disintegrates one of the six things he made and stands at 20, the storage deck first and the laser last, losing the AC and the saves that went with them. No Loaders, no moonshots, no doubles, no Warrior: Talion's rule. The cannons (AC 18, 100 HP) aren't modeled as targets, and the legal stall (Resilient Sphere on himself with the cannons outside) isn't run.
- Alfa (FLLFFL, the lime-green Rock Hard Gladiator) is a Fighter (Battle Master) 13 / Rogue (Assassin) 7 with one homebrew item, the Jet Sword: a +3 katana with a jet in the blunt end, +14 for 1d8+10 and 2d6 fire on every hit, seven cuts in his first round (three, Action Surge's three, and the Jet Lunge). The fire is dealt as its own damage after the steel, so whatever halves a blade halves the blade and not the flame, and Anderson's body counts it. Assassinate, Vex and Studied Attacks mean he nearly always has advantage, which is what his Sneak Attack needs. He has one Reaction and three uses for it, taken as the blows arrive: Parry (Defensive Duelist: +6 against melee until his next turn, when six would stop the blow), Uncanny Dodge (any bullet, or a blade of 20 or more, from someone he can see), and Riposte (a superiority die and a cut back at a blade that missed, with its own Sneak Attack, because it isn't his turn). The rest of the dice go to Precision Attack on near misses, which tested better than saving them. Left out: the shuriken, Cunning Strike, Feinting and Disarming Attack, and losing the sword (nobody here disarms). His code, that he knocks out and doesn't kill, changes nothing in an arena where nobody stays dead.
- V has one Operating System slot and picks before each fight: the Sandevistan by default, the cyberdeck against Jin, Battle Beast and Itachi (where it tested better). `v-sandy` and `v-deck` force one. Against Smasher he takes the Scalpel, because Smasher's sheet says 20 lightning from one source shorts him out (no Reactions, no Sandevistan until the end of his next turn), and Smasher's Counter-Sandevistan answers anyone who speeds up first (Anderson's Haste counts).

## Where the ladder stood on 12 Nov 2026 (300 fights per 1v1 cell, 500 free-for-alls)

1v1, by average win rate: Doom Slayer 93%, Link 89%, Anderson 77%, Smasher 73%, Guy 72%, Kratos 69%, V 61%, Battle Beast 61%, Musashi 55%, Itachi 49%, Jin 44%, Talion 39%, Crane 38%, Guts 38%, Alfa 33%, Dark Link 31%, Arthur 29%, Handsome Jack 28%, Spike 7%, Goblin Slayer 2%.

The massive-damage rule moved Guy up and Jin down: Night Guy used to leave Relentless Rage and Iron Will something to save against.

What breaks the pattern:
- Link and Anderson draw every time: Nayru's Love against a body that keeps getting up.
- Itachi is the upset machine. Tsukuyomi on a low Wisdom save ends a fight before it starts, so he beats the Slayer (74%), Anderson (81%), Battle Beast (93%), Guts (65%) and Talion (73%). He still loses to Smasher (Mage Slayer and the ICE), Arthur and Alfa (who kill him before he casts), Link, and Musashi (Wisdom +9 with advantage against illusions, and three Indomitables at +20).
- Talion v2's Freeflow Counter turns melee hits into misses and feeds his Might, so he beats the melee middle of the ladder: Guts (97%), Spike (100%), Crane (90%), Jin (79%), Battle Beast (68%) and Alfa (88%). Anything that shoots him (Smasher, Arthur) or out-hits his counters (the Slayer, Anderson, Link, Guy, Musashi) still wins.
- Crane v3's claws (2d10+8, ignoring resistance, plus a bite) beat Jin and Guts about three times in four.
- V vs Adam Smasher is close to a coin flip (V about 40%), the fight his whole game builds to.
- Kratos beats Anderson nine times in ten (fire, then the heart) and V every time (V's blades can't get through Rage resistance and the shield Parry). He loses to the Slayer, Link, Battle Beast, Guy and Smasher.
- Musashi is a coin flip overall and almost never one in a given fight. He beats whoever comes at him with a weapon in the hand: Talion (97%), Crane (93%), Jin (77%), Guts (82%), Alfa (81%), and Itachi (94%), whose genjutsu doesn't take. He's the one fighter Arthur can't simply shoot (Arthur wins about two in five), because six cuts a turn cross sixty feet faster than a revolver empties 204 hit points. He loses to what a sword doesn't settle: the Slayer, Link, Anderson, Guy and Smasher nearly every time, Kratos seven times in eight, Battle Beast three in four.
- Dark Link wins what he can copy or roll away from and loses to everything else, which is the Water Temple's lesson: bring the hammer. He beats Talion (87%) and Arthur (91%), Spike and Goblin Slayer every time, and Itachi about three times in five (there's no mind in a shadow for genjutsu to hold). Two hands beat him: Guts five times in six, Jin, Crane's claws and V more than nine in ten. Link beats him every time in three or four rounds, with Deku Nuts, Nayru's Love and the one sword made for it. He never beats the top six. In his own room, translucent for the first minute, he only outlasts Spike and Goblin Slayer; everyone else is through his 224 hit points before he has a blade.
- Handsome Jack is the boss the walkthroughs describe: one of the easier ones, as long as you can get to him. He beats whoever can't: Talion every time (Freeflow Counter answers a blade, and Jack never swings one), Spike and Goblin Slayer every time, Crane three times in five. Arthur shoots through the holograms and wins five in eight; Musashi crosses the sand and wins two in three; Dark Link has nothing to mirror and still wins nearly three in five. Itachi takes him seven times in ten, Battle Beast four in five, and Alfa nine in ten. Guts loses to him once in fifty, and the eight above that (the Slayer, Link, Anderson, Guy, Smasher, Kratos, V and Jin) never have: six returns at 20 hit points are one more turn each for anyone who hits like they do.
- Alfa is an opening. Seven cuts in the first round, nearly all with advantage and each with the flame behind it, finish whoever can be finished in a round or two: Itachi (98%, before the genjutsu), Handsome Jack nine times in ten (seven cuts go through two holograms and what's behind them), Arthur seven in ten, Jin two in three, and Dark Link nearly three in five with both hands on the hilt (one-handed it's one in five). After the opening he's a 217-HP swordsman, and that isn't enough above him: Musashi out-cuts him four times in five, Talion's Freeflow Counter turns the flurry into a full Might bar and wins eight in nine, and whatever hits harder or halves a blade (Guts, Crane, Battle Beast, V, Kratos, Guy, Smasher, the Slayer, Link) loses to him once in thirty at most. Against Anderson he's the other draw: the flame keeps the priest from regrowing round after round and nothing on the sheet can finish him, so three fights in four run the forty rounds and the rest are Anderson's.

Free-for-all with everyone: Link 57%, V 12%, Smasher 6%, Guy 6%. Nobody beats the rest of the roster at once (solo is 0% for everyone; the crowd ends it in the first round).

## Raid bosses

Above the ladder sits the raid tier: everything from past the god-killers up to the moon, built as 5e monsters to fight a party, with Legendary Resistance, Legendary Actions and phases. They stay out of the ladder's matrix and free-for-all.

    python battle.py raid madara                    # the boss vs all twenty at once
    python battle.py raid madara guy+itachi+slayer  # vs a party

- **Madara Uchiha** (`Madara_Uchiha_Raid.pdf`), CR 30 in two phases. Phase 1 is the Edo Tensei body: 700 HP regrowing 50 a turn, never killed by damage (at 0 it's pinned and Phase 2 starts), ended only by a seal: Itachi's Totsuka, through three Legendary Resistances. Phase 2 is the Ten-Tails jinchūriki: 900 HP, spells don't take, nothing under 100 damage in one hit does anything (Night Guy does), no instant kills, and on his fourth turn the Infinite Tsukuyomi takes everyone not under a Susanoo.
- Against the whole ladder he wins every time: the party burns Phase 1 too fast for the seal and can't finish Phase 2 before the moon. Slow parties stall into forty-round draws, and Itachi's seal lands about once in a hundred. Ten-Tails Madara fought on even terms with Six Paths Naruto, who tanks a moon-splitting beam in *The Last*, so the ladder isn't the tier that beats him.
- **Piccolo, Raditz saga** (`Piccolo_Raditz_Saga_Raid.pdf`), CR 30. He destroyed the moon in chapter 208, which is what puts him in the tier. E2: Str 30, Dex 16, Con 30 in moon units, 270 HP, regeneration (10 a turn and three 40-HP Regrowths), a barrage of five ki blasts; the mouth blast; and the Special Beam Cannon: a turn to charge under concentration, then 12d10 through resistance and immunity, for what his blasts can't handle. He'll blow up the moon if an enemy needs it. As an E2 body he wins every ladder one-on-one and beats all twenty at once about 95% of the time; the rest run the forty rounds.
- **Piccolo vs Madara:** raid against raid, Piccolo wins about 85% (3,000 fights). On direct feats he's the bigger hitter and the tougher body (the moon is his on the page; Madara's moon level is a scaling chain), he blows up the moon on his first action, which kills the Infinite Tsukuyomi, and five blasts a turn out-trade the jinchūriki. Madara's wins are his hax: a Sharingan gaze that costs Piccolo a turn (Dragon Ball gives him no defense against genjutsu), Limbo's shadows, and the Truth-Seeking shield.
- **Exponents** (the raid and cosmic rule). Every creature has an exponent, its tier: E1 the ladder, E2 the moon, E3 a planet, and on up. Its Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, HP and damage are written in its own units, so the same exponent plays ordinary 5e; the mind isn't tiered. Across exponents damage converts, x100 per exponent up and /100 per exponent down, dice included (an E2 d10 is a d10x100 to an E1 body), so a 30-point ladder hit on a moon body is 0.3, nothing. The d20 doesn't scale: one exponent apart, the higher creature has advantage on attacks and saves against the lower and the lower has disadvantage; two or more apart, the higher always hits and saves and the lower can't touch it. A technique can carry its own exponent: Guy's Night Guy is an E2 attack (20d12), the one ladder blow that took half of Ten-Tails Madara, as in the manga. Where there are power levels they set the exponent (Raditz-saga Piccolo's 408 is the bottom of E2; every x100 is one more), otherwise the on-page relations do (a fighter who can't touch another is two under it).
- Raid against raid is the strongest version of each, like the ladder, and only in-character abilities count: Madara starts as the jinchūriki (his revival was Black Zetsu's doing), and nobody gets Legendary Resistance (a party-fight device, not anything in canon). Against a party he's the two-phase raid boss: the Edo body is E1, the jinchūriki E2.
- The raid tier is sized to the moon: next up are the Raditz-saga Z fighters (Roshi blew up the moon, and Yamcha by then out-powers him). Planet-level and above (Saiyan saga Vegeta onward, Frieza, Gurren Lagann) would be a third, cosmic division.

## The cosmic division

Sheets only: nothing here is run in the arena, because nothing on the ladder or in the raid tier is within two exponents of them.

- **Frieza** (`Frieza_Cosmic.pdf`), every form from Planet Vegeta to Black Frieza. By power level: first through third form E3, final form E4 (60 and 120 million). By the on-page relations, where Super gives no numbers: Golden about E7 (even with Super Saiyan Blue Goku), Black about E11 (one strike each for Ultra Instinct Goku and Ultra Ego Vegeta). That's the feats reading; the Elder Kai's universe-shattering line, taken literally, would put Golden near E22 and Black near E26. The sheet shows the conversions in real numbers.

## How the code is shaped

- `sim/engine.py`: dice, the `Combatant` base class, `attack()` and `Fight`. Anything one fighter does to another's attack, save or turn is a hook on `Combatant` (listed at the top of the file): `incoming_attack`, `immune`/`ignore_imm`, `answer_standoff`, `retaliate` and the rest. Nothing patches the engine from outside.
- `sim/fighters/`: one file per sheet. Rules that other sheets check are traits on the class (`caster`, `evil`, `evasion`, `never_stunned`, `shrugs_stunning_strike`, `averts_eyes`, `humanoid`, `danger`...), not name lists. Conditions (charmed, frightened, dread, invisible, sapped...) and summons are handled by the engine for everyone.
- `sim/roster.py`: command-line names onto fighters.
- `sim/duels/`: the narrated single-fight loggers from the first bouts, kept for the round-by-round output format. Standalone.

To add a fighter: a new file in `sim/fighters/`, a line in `sim/fighters/__init__.py`, a name in `roster.py`, and the key in `ROSTER` in `battle.py`.

The flattening (Nov 2026) changed no results: on 5,564 seeded fights (every 1v1 pairing, both free-for-alls, every solo and 400 random teams) the old chain and the new layout produced the same winner, rounds, final HP and dice count for every fight. The rule fixes (Izanami, Evasion, Anderson) and the move to one sheet per character came after it, in a separate commit.

## Sheets

    cd sheets/build && python build_slayer.py      # writes ../pdf/Doom_Slayer_5e_Build.pdf

Needs `pip install reportlab pypdf` and the DejaVu fonts (`fonts-dejavu` on Debian/Ubuntu). Each build script is self-contained except the Guy, Slayer, Link, Anderson, Spike, V, Kratos, Musashi, Dark Link, Handsome Jack, Madara, Piccolo and Frieza ones, which `exec` the shared fonts, styles and helpers in `sheet_style.py`. Without the DejaVu fonts (Windows) it falls back to Calibri and Georgia, which is how `V_5e_Build.pdf`, `Kratos_5e_Build.pdf`, `Miyamoto_Musashi_5e_Build.pdf`, `Dark_Link_5e_Build.pdf` and `Handsome_Jack_5e_Build.pdf` were built.
