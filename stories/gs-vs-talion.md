# Goblins

*Goblin Slayer vs Talion.* The arena's run, written up as a story from both sides.

The fight is real, but this matchup isn't close: Talion won all 2,000 runs of `python battle.py 1v1 gs talion`, in under two rounds on average. Freeflow Counter turns Goblin Slayer's sword blows into misses, Hunter's Mark and the Elf-shot do the rest, and the Might bar ends it. This is the longest of 600, picked by `fight_log.py gs talion 600`: three rounds, two Second Winds, and Goblin Slayer's shield rim knocks Talion off his feet twice. In Tolkien, "goblin" and "orc" are the same creature by two names, which is the whole reason these two meet.

**I. Goblin Slayer**

Seven goblins in the first tunnel. Four at the fork. Eleven in the big chamber, and one shaman, who went first.

He counted them as he always did. He counted the ones he'd killed and the ones still breathing, and he counted his knives, his potions and the steps back to the entrance. Counting was how you stayed alive. Goblins were stupid but they weren't careless, and they would learn from a man who was.

These goblins were wrong.

They were too big, for one. Taller than a man, some of them, with black blood and heavy iron armor. They fought in ranks, like soldiers. And every one of them had a mark burned into it somewhere, a glowing white sigil that pulsed like a heartbeat. When he killed them, the mark flickered and went out.

He did not know what it meant. He did not need to know. They were goblins.

He came out of the last chamber into the gray light of a high valley with his sword dripping. There was a man waiting for him there.

The man was tall and lean, with dark hair and a ranger's leathers, a sword on his hip and a bow on his back. Light was coming off him: pale and cold and blue-white, like moonlight on frost. When Goblin Slayer looked at him straight, it was one man. When he looked out of the corner of his visor, there were two: the man, and something standing in the same place, older and taller and crowned.

The man looked past him, into the cave. At the bodies.

"Those were mine," the man said.

Goblin Slayer said nothing.

"Twenty-three of them. Branded. I'd spent a month building that warband." The man's voice was very calm. "Do you know what you've done?"

"Goblins," Goblin Slayer said.

The man went still.

"They were goblins," Goblin Slayer said, "and now they are dead."

**II. Talion**

*He's mortal*, Celebrimbor said in his head. *Barely armored. A child with a sword.*

"He just killed twenty-three Uruks," Talion said under his breath. "Alone."

*Orcs. Not Uruks. Snagas and a few hunters, and one shaman. We can raise more.*

Talion wasn't listening. He was looking at the man in front of him: the dented steel helm, the visor slit with nothing visible behind it, the cheap mail, and the battered little round shield strapped to his arm. He'd killed twenty-three of Talion's soldiers, and he was standing there afterward like a farmer who'd finished weeding a field.

He'd seen men like that before. He'd been one, on the Black Gate, before the Black Hand came. He'd been a ranger who did his job and went home to Ioreth and Dirhael, and never asked what the job was making of him.

Then the Black Hand came, and the job didn't matter anymore. Only the hunting did.

"Who did they take from you?" Talion asked quietly.

The man in the helm was silent for a very long time.

"My sister," he said.

Talion closed his eyes.

"Mine took my wife," he said. "And my son."

The two of them stood in the gray light. Behind Talion, the wraith flickered.

"Walk away," Talion said. "Those weren't the ones who took her. Walk away, and I'll forget this."

"No," said Goblin Slayer. "All goblins must die."

He drew his sword.

**III. Goblin Slayer**

He moved first. He always moved first.

He had seen the man's stance and the bow on his back, and knew it was better not to let him use it. He closed the distance in three quick steps and cut low at the knee, the way he'd cut a hundred goblin hamstrings.

The man was simply not there.

He was beside him instead, and his hand was on Goblin Slayer's sword arm, *turning it*. The blade went wide, and the man's elbow came back into his helm hard enough to ring it like a bell.

He cut again, and the man turned it again. A third time, a fourth. Each time the man moved like water, and each time something hit Goblin Slayer back.

*He reads blows*, Goblin Slayer thought. *Very well. Do not give him blows to read.*

He took the fifth cut short, a feint, and on the sixth he drove the iron rim of his shield into the man's chest instead of his sword. The rim went in under the ribs. The man's feet went out from under him, and he hit the ground on his back.

Goblin Slayer's blade went in after it: once along the ribs, then once again, deep.

*Good.*

**IV. Talion**

He hadn't been hit like that in a long time.

Not since Mordor. Not since the Black Hand's knife went across his throat on the Black Gate. He lay on the cold stone for a heartbeat with the taste of copper in his mouth, the man in the helmet standing over him, and he was almost *grateful*.

*Get up*, Celebrimbor said. *Finish this.*

He got up.

The Might came up through him like fire. It was the wraith's power and his own anger braided together, the thing that had let him walk through Mordor's armies for years. He drew Urfael and went in.

The first blow went through the cheap mail like paper and opened the man's chest. The second took him across the arm and the third across the thigh. Then four more, faster, the blade a blur of pale blue light. He felt each one land. He felt the man stagger.

When he stepped back, Goblin Slayer was on one knee, and blood was running out of his helm.

**V. Goblin Slayer**

*Eight wounds. Three deep.*

He counted them, the way he counted everything. He was down to perhaps a quarter of himself. He reached into his pouch for a potion, and the familiar bitter taste of the stamina draught hit the back of his throat. It burned and steadied.

He stood.

*The man is not a goblin*, he thought. *He fights like no goblin. He reads blows. He bleeds light.*

He was something else, then, and Goblin Slayer did not fight something else. He fought goblins.

But the man stood between him and the goblins, and that made him a problem. Problems could be solved.

He feinted high, cut low, and drove the shield in again. The man went down again, and this time the short sword went into the side under the ribs, deep. The man grunted, the first sound he'd made.

Then the man was up again with the bow in his hands. Goblin Slayer had not seen him draw it.

The arrows were made of light.

Three of them hit him in a row: the chest, the shoulder, the hip. They did not stop in him. They went straight through, and every one of them burned.

**VI. Talion**

*He keeps getting up*, Celebrimbor said. There was something almost like respect in it.

"So do I," Talion said.

He drew Azkar again. The man was swaying and his shield arm was hanging low. He'd lost so much blood that his armor was black with it. But he came forward anyway, sword up, short careful steps, the way he must have walked into a hundred dark caves.

*Ioreth*, Talion thought. *Dirhael.*

The Black Hand had taken them in front of him, and there had been nothing he could do. He'd spent every day since then hunting the thing that did it. He'd become something that couldn't die, and he'd lost a little more of himself every year.

He looked at the man in the helmet, and saw a man who'd been doing the same thing for ten years. The man was doing it without a wraith or a ring or anything at all but a cheap sword, a little shield, and counting.

He felt very tired.

Three arrows. Each one hit, and the man still didn't go down.

Talion stepped into the wraith-world, went across the valley in a flash of pale light, and came out behind him. He struck once with Urfael, across the back of the knees, and the man fell.

This time he didn't get up.

**VII. Goblin Slayer**

He was lying in the stones, and the sky was very gray.

He could not feel his legs. He tried to count his wounds and lost track at fourteen.

*I have failed.*

He waited for the fear, and it did not come. There was only the familiar flat quiet that he'd always lived in, ever since the day he hid under the floorboards and listened.

The man knelt beside him. The cold light coming off him was dimmer now, flickering.

"I won't kill you," the man said.

Goblin Slayer did not answer.

"Go home," the man said. "Wherever home is. Hunt your goblins." He paused. "Not mine."

Goblin Slayer thought of the farm, and the girl with red hair who left the lamp lit for him every night, though he never asked her to. He thought of the priestess and the elf, the dwarf and the lizardman. He thought of the guild girl's smile when he turned in a quest.

"They are goblins," he said. His voice was a rasp.

The man laughed, very softly. It might have been the first time in years.

"Yes," Talion said. "I suppose they are."

He stood, and the wraith flickered behind him. He walked away into the gray light, and the valley was empty.

**VIII. Goblin Slayer**

It was three days before he could stand, and a week before he could walk back down the valley. He did it slowly, counting steps.

When he reached the guild, the guild girl took one look at him and went white.

"What happened? A goblin lord? A champion?"

"No," Goblin Slayer said. He set his helm on the counter. It was dented in three places he hadn't noticed during the fight.

"Something else," he said.

That night, at the farm, he sat by the fire with a whetstone and his sword and did what he always did after a quest. He thought it through: what had gone wrong, and what he would do next time.

*He reads blows*, Goblin Slayer thought. *So do not give him blows. Give him fire. Give him a cave with one entrance, and the smoke, and a flood.*

The girl with red hair brought him stew and sat across the table from him without saying anything, the way she always did.

He ate.

Somewhere far away, in a land with a black sky, a man made of light and grief was building another army of goblins.

Goblin Slayer sharpened his sword and kept counting.
