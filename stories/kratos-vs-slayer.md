# The Rage That Does Not End

*Kratos vs the Doom Slayer.* The bar again, and a trial.

The fight is real: the most dramatic of 400 runs of `python battle.py 1v1 kratos slayer`, picked by `fight_log.py kratos slayer 400`. The Slayer wins about 99% of the time. Kratos's opening here is as good as it gets (222 damage through the Sentinel armor's temporary HP), and the BFG answers it. Relentless Rage holds twice. The Crucible misses twice, and then the third swing crits for 147. Kratos climbs out of Hades at 1 HP, and the Crucible's own rule, that anything it leaves at 150 or fewer dies, ends him anyway.

## The Bar

**Kratos**

The bar was small for a man his size. He had to duck under the lintel, and the stool groaned when he sat.

There was a glass on the counter with a ring of milk dried at the bottom, and beside it a small cup that smelled of rice wine. Two cigarette ends lay in an ashtray. Others had been here. Others would come.

The bartender set down a cup of mead without being asked. Kratos looked at it, and then at the old man.

"I did not order this."

"It's paid for," the bartender said.

Kratos drank. It was good mead, Asgardian, or close to it. That troubled him, but he had stopped expecting the worlds to make sense a long time ago.

The door opened.

The man who came through it was as large as Kratos, and he made the doorway look small too. He was armored head to foot in green plate, scarred and scorched, with something faintly glowing in the seams. His helmet had a narrow visor of gold glass, and nothing of his face showed behind it. He moved without sound. Every step was placed, the way a hunter's steps are placed.

He smelled of brimstone, and under it of something Kratos knew very well. It was the smell of a battlefield after the battle, when nothing on it is still alive.

The armored man sat two stools down. The bartender set a glass in front of him. He did not drink it. He did not take off his helmet.

They sat.

Kratos had sat in silence with many beings. With Mimir, who could not stop talking, so the silence was always Kratos's alone. With Atreus, when the boy was young and the silences were full of everything neither of them knew how to say. With Freya, after.

This silence was different. It was the silence of two animals at the same watering hole, each deciding whether the other was prey.

"You hunt," Kratos said at last.

The helmet turned toward him.

"Demons," Kratos said. "I can smell it on you. Their blood. Their fire." He turned his cup. "Many of them."

The armored man said nothing. After a moment, he nodded once.

"Why?"

The helmet stayed on him for a long time. Then the armored man reached into a compartment on his belt, took out something small and flat, and set it on the counter between them.

It was a photograph, worn soft and creased from handling. In it was a rabbit, small and brown and white, sitting in someone's hands.

Kratos looked at the photograph for a long moment.

He thought of Lysandra and Calliope. He thought of a temple, and Ares's laughter, and the ash that still clung to his skin after all these centuries.

"They took her from you," he said.

The armored man put the photograph away. He did not answer. He did not need to.

"Then you have reason," Kratos said.

They sat a while longer. Then the armored man stood, and looked at Kratos, and tilted his helmet toward the door.

Kratos understood. He had seen that look on a hundred faces in Greece: the look of a warrior who has smelled a god and wants to know whether it bleeds. He had worn that look himself, once, in another life, for a very long time.

"I do not fight for that anymore," Kratos said quietly. "I have spent too long being that. I chose to be something else."

The armored man did not move.

Kratos sighed. He thought of Týr's trials in Valhalla, and four fights against the god of war in four armors. He thought of a throne, and a boy in Spartan armor who was himself, asking whether he had changed.

*Perhaps this is another trial*, he thought. *Perhaps they never end.*

He finished his mead and stood. The stool groaned with relief.

"Very well," said Kratos.

## The Trial

**Kratos**

Sand and sky and silence, and the armored man forty paces away.

Kratos reached into himself for Spartan Rage. It came, as it always came, from the deep red place he had spent a lifetime trying to wall up. It had changed in Valhalla. It was not fire anymore. It was gold.

The Blade of Olympus formed in his hand, the blade he had buried in himself to give mankind hope. He felt the old weight of it. In his other hand the Leviathan Axe was cold as a winter sea.

He charged.

The first blow took the armored man in the chest. The shield caught him under the chin and *lifted* him off his feet, and then Kratos was on him, axe and blade. It was not rage, not anymore. It was rhythm. Frost and gold and the old Spartan forms. Six blows, and the armor cracked and smoked. Something glowing poured out of the seams and was gone.

The armored man hit the sand on his back.

*It is done*, Kratos thought. *Boy, I*—

The armored man was standing up.

**The Slayer**

Down.

Up.

The god was strong. Stronger than the Marauders, stronger than the Titans, stronger than the Icon's first form. The god had cut through the Sentinel plating like it was nothing.

The Slayer did not care.

The Rage came, the thing from Hell's deepest pit that had never stopped burning in him. It washed the pain out of his body like water off a fire. He brought the BFG up off his back and fired.

The world went green.

The ball of plasma hit the god in the chest and threw him the length of a city block, bending and burning his armor. The Slayer did not wait. He fired again. The second shot hit harder than the first, and the god went down into the sand and the sand went to glass around him.

The Slayer stood in the green afterglow and waited to see if the god would get up.

The god got up.

**Kratos**

*That* was a weapon.

He had faced Zeus's lightning, Thor's hammer, the Hydra and the Titans and the fury of Olympus. Nothing had ever hit him like that. It was not lightning, not fire, not ice. It was something greater. The essence of destruction itself, made into a weapon by mortal hands.

He rose. His armor was smoking. One of his gauntlets had melted to his arm.

*He is like you were*, said something in him. It might have been Mimir's voice, or Faye's, or his own. *He is what you were. The thing that does not stop.*

"I know," Kratos said aloud.

He went in again.

The axe caught the armored man in the shoulder, and the Blade of Olympus in the ribs. He called on the frost and brought it down: Winter's Bite, cold enough to stop a heart. The armored man staggered, then fired that weapon again. Kratos took the third shot full in the chest and felt something break inside.

He breathed. He called on his Valor, and the gold rage closed his wounds.

He was not finished.

**The Slayer**

The Slayer reached back and drew the Crucible.

Red light. Argent fire, from the heart of Hell itself, made into a blade. It had cut through the Icon of Sin. It had killed the Khan Maykr. It had slain priests and Hell lords and every abomination that stood between him and what he hunted.

He swung.

The god turned it on the shield.

**Kratos**

It was a red blade, crackling, and it *screamed* when it moved.

Kratos caught it on the Guardian Shield and felt the shock all the way to his teeth. He had parried Zeus. He had parried Baldur. He knew a killing blow when one came at him.

He answered with the axe, and the armored man went down.

He got up.

Kratos stared at him.

The man came at him with that red blade again. Kratos turned it again. He put the man down again with the shield's edge, the Blade of Olympus and the axe, everything he had. The armored man hit the sand hard enough to crater it.

He got up.

Kratos looked at him and finally understood. This was not a warrior. This was the *rage itself*, given a body and a gun and a reason. It did not stop because there was nothing in it that knew how to stop.

He had been this. He had been this for a very long time. Then he had a son, and learned to be something else.

The armored man raised the red blade a third time.

**The Slayer**

Third swing.

The Slayer did not think about it. He never thought about it. He did what he did, and he did it until there was nothing left to do it to.

The Crucible came down.

This time the god did not turn it.

It went through the shield. It went through the god's armor and through the god, and the Slayer felt the Argent fire *eat* into him. He felt it the way it ate demons: hungrily, completely.

The god fell.

**Kratos**

He was in the dark.

He knew this dark. He'd been here before. He'd climbed out of it three times now, out of the Underworld, out of Hades' realm, out of a place no mortal or god returned from. Each time he'd crawled up the cliff with his bare hands, through the rivers and the shades, and the dead could not hold him.

He began to climb.

He reached the light, and the sand, and the white sky. He opened his eyes and drew one breath.

The red blade was still in him.

He looked down at it. The light from it was eating into his armor and his flesh, and deeper than that. It was not cutting. It was *unmaking*.

*Ah*, Kratos thought. *This is a different kind of death.*

He looked up. The armored man stood over him, his gold visor reflecting Kratos's own face. It was gray and ash-pale and very tired.

"Your rage," Kratos said. His voice was barely a rasp. "It does not end."

The armored man said nothing.

"It will not," Kratos said. "Unless you let it." He breathed once, very slowly. "I am sorry. For your rabbit."

The light went out.

**The Slayer**

The Slayer stood in the sand for a long time.

The Crucible's light faded. He sheathed it.

He looked down at the god for a while. Then he knelt and, carefully, the way he did very few things, took the god's axe and laid it across his chest. He took the god's hands and folded them over the haft.

He stood. He did not say anything. There was no one to say it to.

He walked back to the bar.

## After

The bar was empty when the Slayer came in. The bartender set a glass down in front of him. He did not drink it.

He sat for a long time.

It was late in the night, though he could not have said how he knew, when the door opened again.

Kratos ducked under the lintel. He was gray with ash from head to foot, and there was dirt under his nails and on his knees, the kind that comes from climbing. His armor was gone. There was a new scar across his chest, red and raw, in the shape of a blade.

He sat down heavily on the stool next to the Slayer. The bartender set down a cup of mead.

Kratos drank it in one swallow.

"Four," Kratos said.

The Slayer turned his helmet toward him.

"Four times," Kratos said. "Out of the Underworld." He set down the cup. "Once a day, it seems. Your blade cannot hold me past a night's rest."

The Slayer looked at him for a long moment.

Then he reached up, slowly, and pushed his glass across the counter to Kratos.

Kratos looked at the glass, and at the Slayer.

He drank it.

They sat in silence after that. It was not the silence of two animals at a watering hole. It was a different silence, the kind between two old soldiers who have each finally met someone who understands.

Somewhere outside, very far off, demons were screaming. The Slayer could hear them. He always could.

Before he rose, he set the photograph of the rabbit on the counter for a moment, so that Kratos could see it again.

Then he put it away, nodded once, and went out into the dark to hunt.
