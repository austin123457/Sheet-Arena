# Two Men Who Should Be Dead

*Spike Spiegel vs Arthur Morgan.* The arena's run, written up as a story from both sides.

The fight is real: the most dramatic of the fights Spike won in 3,000 runs of `python battle.py 1v1 spike arthur`, picked by `fight_log.py spike arthur 3000 Spike`. This is the upset: Arthur wins about 96% of the time, because Dead Eye's first round is ten shots with advantage, and Spike spends all six Luck Points in it and still eats nine. Here Spike held on, healed every round, came back from 0 on Nine Lives, and landed the one Stunning Strike he needed.

**I. Arthur**

The other fella was already smoking when Arthur got there.

He was skinny, in a blue suit that hung on him like it had been slept in a few times, with a yellow shirt and a mess of green-black hair. He stood loose, his weight on nothing in particular, and he looked at Arthur like a man reading a menu he'd already decided to order nothing from.

Arthur fished a cigarette out of his own coat, patted his pockets, and came up empty for a match.

The fella held out a lighter without a word.

"Obliged," Arthur said. The flame caught, and he breathed in. The cough came after, the way it always did lately: deep and wet and rattling around in the bottom of him. He turned his head and spat into the sand and didn't look at what came up.

"That doesn't sound good," the fella said.

"No," Arthur agreed. "It don't."

They smoked for a little while. Arthur didn't know where this place was. Open sand, a sky too wide and too blue, no horses, no Dutch, no John. Just a distance marked out between two men, and an understanding about what the distance was for.

"You look like a man who's done some things," Arthur said eventually.

The fella smiled at that, a lazy smile that didn't go anywhere near his eyes. One of them didn't quite match the other.

"Haven't we all."

"Some more'n others."

The fella flicked the end of his cigarette into the sand and walked off toward his mark. He didn't hurry, and he didn't look back.

Arthur watched him go and thought: *that's a man who already knows how he's going to die, and he's just waiting to see if it's today.*

He knew the look. He saw it in the mirror these days.

**II. Spike**

Sixty feet.

The cowboy had a heavy coat and a hat, a face like a weathered fence post, and a revolver on each hip. He stood the way men stand when they've been in more gunfights than conversations. Spike could tell from how his hands hung, still and close and patient.

*Fast*, Spike thought. *He's going to be very fast.*

So Spike was faster.

The Jericho was in his hand before the cowboy's fingers had finished twitching. Two shots: the first one punched through the heavy coat high on the shoulder, and the second clipped his ribs. Then Spike was moving, zigzagging across the sand, and he was in close with fists before the cowboy had cleared leather.

The cowboy turned his shoulder into the first two punches like a man who'd been in a lot of bar fights. The third caught him on the jaw.

Not bad. Not enough.

Then the cowboy drew.

**III. Arthur**

The world went slow.

It always did. The color drained out of everything until the sand was the color of old paper, the sky was the color of tin, and the man in blue was moving like he was underwater. Arthur could hear his own heart, slow and heavy as a church bell.

He painted the fella. One red mark, then two, then six, one on every place a bullet could go.

Then he let the world go fast again and fired all of them.

The fella in blue *moved*. Arthur had never seen anything like it. He wasn't fast like a gunslinger. He was loose, like water poured out of a bucket, flowing around the bullets. One of them simply wasn't there when the bullet arrived. Arthur saw him catch another on his forearm and turn it aside, like a man brushing away a fly.

But six was a lot of bullets, and Arthur had more.

He thumbed the hammer and fired again, four more, his hands moving on their own the way they had since he was fourteen. When the smoke cleared, the man in blue was on one knee in the sand, with blood soaking through his yellow shirt in three places.

"Sorry, friend," Arthur said, and he even meant it.

**IV. Spike**

*Ow.*

He'd used up all his luck. He could feel it, the same way you can feel the last dollar leave your wallet. Every lucky step and lucky lean, everything that had kept him alive through the cathedral and the monorail and a hundred bounties that went sideways, he'd spent all of it in about four seconds. He'd still taken nine bullets.

Spike breathed.

*Be like water.* In through the nose, down into the belly, and out. He felt the hurt settle, and his breathing steadied as the bleeding slowed. It wasn't healing exactly, but close enough to keep moving.

He got up.

The cowboy looked surprised. Spike liked that.

He went back in, two shots and then three strikes, a knee and an elbow and a palm to the chest that knocked the hat clean off. The cowboy took them all and stayed standing, and fired back.

It went like that for a while.

Spike would breathe, and come in, and hit him. The cowboy would cough that terrible cough, stand up straighter, and fire back. Every time Spike thought he was out of gas, he found a little more. He could see the cowboy finding a little more too, somewhere behind that cough.

They were both running on empty, and neither one was willing to say so.

**V. Arthur**

He was tired.

Lord, he was tired. His chest felt like it was full of wet sand, and every breath tasted like copper. The fella in blue was a mess of blood and bruises and he *kept coming*, and Arthur couldn't for the life of him figure out what was holding him up.

*What're you fighting for, son?*

He thought about Eliza and Isaac, and the little grave he'd visited too few times. He thought about Mary, and the letters he'd never answered right. He thought about Thomas Downes coughing on his porch while Arthur beat him for a debt he couldn't pay.

He'd spent his whole life fighting for men who didn't deserve it. He'd been a bad man for a very long time.

Arthur drew again.

Four shots. The man in blue caught the first one on his arm, the way he always did, but the next three went in. The last one hit him square in the chest and he went down, flat on his back in the sand, and he didn't move.

Arthur let out a breath. It came out ragged.

"I'm sorry," he said, and he meant that too.

Then the man in blue sat up.

**VI. Spike**

He'd been here before.

It was the cathedral again: the stained glass blowing out around him, the long fall, the dark. Somewhere far away, Julia was singing.

*Not yet.* It wasn't a decision, really. It was more like his body simply hadn't been informed that it was dead, and he wasn't about to be the one to tell it.

He had one hit point's worth of life left in him. He didn't know the number, but he could feel it, a candle in a high wind.

Spike stood up, and smiled at the cowboy, and thought: *well, that's that, then.*

He went in for what he knew would be the last time.

He fired the Jericho twice, both clean, then crossed the distance. The cowboy was raising the Schofield again, slower now, the cough eating him from the inside. Spike stepped inside the gun and *struck*.

He put everything into it, a single punch straight to the center of the chest, the way his first teacher taught him a lifetime ago on Mars. It wasn't a strike to hurt. It was a strike to *stop*.

The cowboy's eyes went wide, and his whole body locked up.

He fell backward into the sand like a tree.

**VII. Arthur**

He couldn't move.

He could see the sky, wide and blue and empty. He could feel the sand against the back of his neck, but his arms wouldn't answer, and his legs wouldn't answer, and the Schofield lay a foot from his hand and might as well have been in Saint Denis.

*So this is how it goes.*

He wasn't scared. He'd thought he would be, but he wasn't. He was just tired, and a little sad, and somewhere underneath it relieved, the way a man is relieved when he finally sets down something heavy he's been carrying too long.

The man in blue stood over him, swaying. Blood was dripping from his chin, and that strange mismatched eye was looking down at him.

"You did good, son," Arthur tried to say. He wasn't sure it came out.

**VIII. Spike**

He hit him once more, clean and quick. He didn't do it out of cruelty. It's just what you do at the end of a fight, so the other man doesn't have to keep doing this.

Then Spike sat down in the sand next to the cowboy, because his legs had stopped arguing.

He dug in his jacket for a cigarette. The pack was crushed and soaked through with blood, but one of them was still mostly dry. He lit it with shaking hands, took one drag, and then, after a second, leaned over and put it between the cowboy's lips.

The cowboy's chest rose, and the cigarette's tip glowed. That was all he had the strength for.

They sat like that for a while, two men who should've been dead a long time ago, on a stretch of sand under a sky that didn't belong to either of them.

"Hey, cowboy," Spike said, eventually.

The cowboy's eyes moved to him.

"You ever get the feeling you're just..." Spike waved his hand vaguely at the sky. "Dreaming? Like none of it's quite real, and you're waiting to wake up?"

The cowboy's mouth moved. It might have been a smile.

Spike looked up at the sky, and pointed a finger at it like a gun.

"Bang," he said quietly.

Then he lay back in the sand, closed his eyes, and waited to see if he'd wake up.
