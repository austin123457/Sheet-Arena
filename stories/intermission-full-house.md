# Intermission: Full House

*No fight yet.* Picks up straight after [The Regulars](intermission-the-regulars.md): the steaming glass at the end of the bar finds its owner, and the room keeps filling.

---

**Smasher**

SYSTEM REBOOT.

CORE INTEGRITY: 100%. ARMOR: 100%. ORDNANCE: 100%.

LOCATION: UNKNOWN.

He was standing outside a door. It was wooden, old and badly fitted, with yellow lamplight leaking around the frame. His last log entry was a red mark painted on his own chest plate from forty feet away, and then nothing. His systems reported no damage. His systems also reported a timestamp that did not exist.

He opened the door. He had to turn sideways and duck to get through it.

THERMAL: 5 ORGANICS. 1 UNCLASSIFIED (BARTENDER).

TARGET: MORGAN, ARTHUR. *Neutralized prior.* ERROR: TARGET ACTIVE.

TARGET: SPIEGEL, SPIKE. *Neutralized prior.* ERROR: TARGET ACTIVE.

TARGET: UNKNOWN. Male, organic, priest's vestments. Regenerative tissue detected. *Anomalous.*

TARGET: UNKNOWN. Male, organic, prosthetic left arm with an integrated cannon. Blade, 200 kg, est. *Anomalous.*

The four organics at the counter had gone very still. The one with the giant blade had his hand on the hilt. The priest was smiling, but his hands had disappeared into his coat. The cowboy's hand rested near his revolver. The skinny one hadn't moved at all except to take the cigarette out of his mouth.

Smasher's arm-mounted cannon began to spin up, a rising whine that filled the little room.

At the end of the bar, the old man set down the glass he was polishing.

It made a small sound. *Click.*

"Not in here," the bartender said.

Smasher's targeting software tried to lock onto the old man. It failed. It tried again and returned a string of characters that weren't any language Smasher's systems recognized. The cannon spun down on its own.

He did not order it to. It just stopped.

The old man went back to polishing.

Smasher stood there for a long moment, recalculating. Then he walked to the end of the bar where the steaming glass was waiting and sat down on a stool that had not been there a moment ago. It was large enough, and it was reinforced steel.

He lifted the glass. Coolant, Arasaka military grade, the exact formulation. It was chilled to the correct temperature.

He drank.

---

**Arthur**

The machine drank a glass of whatever-it-was without a word, and set it down, and the bartender filled it again.

Nobody said anything for a while.

The big swordsman had taken his hand off his sword, slowly. The priest's hands had come back out of his coat. Spike had put the cigarette back in his mouth. Arthur found his own hand was still resting by the Schofield, and he made himself move it.

"Morgan," the machine said.

Arthur looked up. The voice was low and grinding, like gravel in a mill.

"You know my name?"

The red eyes turned toward him. There were two of them again. Arthur was fairly sure he'd shot one of them out.

"I knew another Morgan," the machine said. "Morgan Blackhand. On the roof of Arasaka Tower, fifty-four years ago. You shoot like him." A pause. "I outlived him too."

"Well," Arthur said, after a moment. "I ain't him."

"No," the machine agreed. "He did not cough."

Spike laughed out loud. He actually choked on his smoke, and had to bang his own chest. The priest grinned into his drink. Even the big swordsman's mouth twitched at one corner.

Arthur scowled at all of them.

"Funny," he said. "Real funny."

---

**Jin**

The door opened, and Jin Sakai came in out of the rain. He was wiping the water from his face, and the young man in green was behind him.

They had arrived together, somehow. They had walked out of the bar after the duel, and the door had led Jin to the coast of Tsushima and Link to a green field under a sky with two moons. Then they had both walked through other doors, at other times, and found themselves on the same wet street in a city neither recognized, looking at the same yellow light. It seemed the bar did this.

Jin stopped just inside the doorway.

The room was full. There was a long-coated priest, and a scarred giant with a sword that put Jin's whole sense of proportion in question. There was a cowboy coughing into his fist, and a lean man in a blue suit. And at the end of the bar, taking up three stools' worth of space and drinking something blue that steamed, was a thing made of steel and red light.

Jin's hand went to his katana.

Behind him, Link's went to the Master Sword.

"Not in here," said the bartender, without looking up.

Jin looked at the old man. Then at the steel thing. The steel thing looked back at him, unblinking, with a soft whir of focusing lenses.

Jin took his hand off his sword and bowed to the room.

"Jin Sakai," he said. "Of Tsushima."

The man in the blue suit raised his glass. "Spike."

"Arthur Morgan," said the cowboy.

"Alexander Anderson," said the priest, warmly. "A pleasure, friend."

The big one with the sword said nothing. Then, when everyone looked at him: "Guts."

The steel thing said nothing at all.

Link stepped up beside Jin, looked around the room once, and nodded to everyone at the same time.

"Link," Jin offered, on his behalf.

Link nodded again.

The bartender was already setting down a cup of sake and a glass of milk.

---

**Link**

The priest was staring at his sword.

Link was used to people staring at the Master Sword. It did that. But the priest was staring at it the way some people stared at the Temple of Time: with something in his face that was almost hunger and almost tears.

"That blade," the priest said quietly. "It's *holy*."

Link considered this.

"Yes," he said.

"May I?" The priest held out his hands, palms up. "Not to take it. I'd never. Only to be near it."

Link looked at him for a moment. Then he drew the Master Sword, turned it, and laid it flat across the bar between them.

The priest looked down at it. Then he very slowly reached out and put two fingers on the flat of the blade, and closed his eyes.

"*Oh*," he said softly. That was all. He held his fingers there for a long time.

Link waited.

When the priest finally took his hand away, he smiled at Link with a face that was wet.

"Thank you, son," he said. "Thank you."

Link nodded and sheathed it again.

The cowboy coughed, hard, and bent over the bar. Link watched him ride it out and saw what was on his hand afterward when he wiped it.

He reached into his pack and took out a bottle: milk, the good kind, from the ranch with the girl who sang. He set it in front of the cowboy.

The cowboy looked at the bottle, then at Link.

"Milk?"

"Helps," Link said.

The cowboy looked at him for a long moment. Then he uncorked it and drank half the bottle in one swallow. He set it down, blinked, and took a breath, a long one, all the way down. The cough didn't come back after it.

"Well, I'll be," he said. "Where'd you say this came from?"

Link thought about the ranch. About Malon singing, and Epona running, and a field that only existed in seven years that never happened.

"Far," he said.

---

**Kratos**

He was the last to come.

He had to duck under the lintel, and when he straightened up, he was looking at the fullest room he'd seen since Valhalla's feast hall.

He knew none of them. And he knew all of them.

Not their faces. He knew their *kind*. He had seen the swordsman's kind on a thousand battlefields: a soldier who was more scar than skin, who no longer remembered how to sleep. He had seen the cowboy's kind dying in Sparta, men who knew it was coming and faced it anyway. The priest he recognized as a servant of some god who believed in that god completely, as Kratos once had served Ares.

And the steel thing at the end of the bar, drinking something blue. That he did not know, and did not wish to.

He sat down at the only empty stool, which was next to the priest. The bartender set down a cup of mead.

The priest turned to look at him, and his smile stopped.

"You're no man," the priest said slowly.

Kratos drank.

"I can feel it on you," the priest said. "Divinity. Old divinity." His hand had drifted toward his coat again. "You're a *pagan god*."

"I was," said Kratos.

"There is one God," said the priest, very quietly.

Kratos set down his cup and looked at him.

"I have killed several," he said.

The silence in the bar was absolute.

Then the priest threw back his head and *laughed*. It was an enormous laugh that shook the bottles behind the bar, and he slapped the counter so hard his glass jumped.

"*Ha!*" he roared. "*Oh, that's good. That's very good!*" He wiped his eyes. "Well. You'll forgive me if I don't drink to that."

Kratos looked at him, unsmiling.

"Drink to what you like," he said.

The priest grinned and raised his glass anyway.

"To killing false gods, then," he said. "We'll argue about which ones another night."

Kratos considered this. He raised his cup an inch and drank.

At the other end of the bar, the steel thing's lenses were whirring as it looked at him.

TARGET, Kratos imagined it saying. UNCLASSIFIABLE.

He did not look back.

---

**Spike**

It was getting crowded.

Spike leaned back on his stool and looked down the length of the bar. It was longer than it had been. He was sure of that. It had grown to fit everyone without making a fuss about it, the way a good bar does.

At one end, Adam Smasher sat drinking coolant, watching all of them with his red eyes, his cannon spun down and his fingers tapping on the bar like he was counting.

Then Arthur, with his bottle of milk, actually breathing easy for once.

Then Spike himself.

Then Guts, quiet, hands around his cup. He'd moved a little closer to Kratos, and the two of them were looking at each other's weapons with the frank professional interest of two men who'd each carried something enormous for a very long time.

Then Anderson, still chuckling at something. And Kratos, unsmiling, drinking his mead, nodding at whatever Guts had just said very quietly.

Then Jin and Link at the far end, sitting side by side in a comfortable silence, the swordsman from the island and the hero nobody remembered.

Eight people. Every one of them had done something terrible, or been through something terrible, or both. Every one of them had come back from somewhere they shouldn't have.

"Hey," Spike said, to nobody in particular. "Anybody know what we're all here for?"

Nobody answered.

But the bartender put down the glass he was polishing, and for the first time since any of them had come in, he walked out from behind the counter.

He went to the wall by the door, where there was a chalkboard nobody had noticed before. It was a big one, black slate in a wooden frame, the kind saloons used for prices and churches used for hymn numbers. It was blank.

He picked up a piece of chalk.

Every head in the bar turned to watch.

Even Smasher's.

The old man wrote one word across the top, in careful block capitals:

**TONIGHT:**

Then he paused with the chalk against the slate, as if deciding.

The whole bar held its breath.

The chalk began to move.
