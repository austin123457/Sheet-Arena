# Come Unto Me

*Alexander Anderson vs Guts.* The bar, a priest, a man who has a quarrel with God, and the long way back.

The fight is real: the most dramatic of 400 runs of `python battle.py 1v1 anderson guts`, picked by `fight_log.py anderson guts 400`. Anderson wins all of them, because Guts has no fire and no radiant to stop him regrowing, and a man who gets up at 30 HP every turn outlasts anything that can't. But Guts cuts him down in ten of the fifteen rounds. His first turn does 197. Relentless Rage brings him back four times, and he rolls a natural 20 on a death save with two failures already marked. The Nail never comes out: Anderson only reaches for it against a foe who's still more than half whole. What happens after the last blow is the story's: his sheet gives him Lay on Hands, 100 HP, "the orphans, not himself."

## The Bar

**Guts**

He'd woken up here once before.

He remembered a green blur, fists like falling stars, and a dark he didn't come back from. Then a wooden counter under his cheek, a lamp, and an old bartender setting down a cup of something that smelled like Godo's forge wine. There'd been no explanation. The old man just said "it's paid for" and went back to his glasses, and when Guts walked out the door, he was somewhere else.

Now he was back. The armor was gone and his cloak was over his shoulders. The Dragonslayer was leaning against the bar beside him, where it had no business being, in a room this small.

The Brand on his neck was quiet. That was the strangest part. It was always bleeding, always crawling. Here it was quiet, like a dog asleep by a fire.

He drank.

The door opened, and a priest came in.

He was a big man, nearly as big as Guts, in a long gray coat over a priest's black cassock. A silver cross hung on his chest. He had round glasses and short pale hair, and a scar down his cheek. He had the kind of face that smiles at children and means it.

Guts's hand went to the Dragonslayer.

He'd seen a man like that once. Mozgus, the inquisitor, a priest with a book in one hand and a hot iron in the other, in a town full of burned women who'd confessed to anything. He'd watched people pray on their knees while their neighbors burned. He'd watched the Holy See's knights drag Casca to the stake.

The priest sat down two stools away. The bartender put a glass of something in front of him. He picked it up, said a quiet word over it, and drank.

Then he looked at Guts's neck, and his smile went away.

**Anderson**

*The mark of the Beast.*

He knew it the moment he saw it. A sigil burned into the flesh at the base of the neck, crusted with blood, old and new. It was a thing made by no human hand. It was faintly warm, in a way that had nothing to do with skin. There was a smell to it. Brimstone, and something older.

Alexander Anderson had spent his life hunting the things that smelled like that. Vampires, ghouls, demons wearing men's faces. He had cut them apart with blessed steel and burned their ashes and given thanks for every one.

He set down his glass.

"That mark," he said. "Where did you get it?"

The swordsman didn't look at him. "Some gods gave it to me."

"There is one God," said Anderson.

"Then I've got a quarrel with him."

Anderson studied the man. He was scarred everywhere, one eye gone, one arm iron. The sword leaning on the bar beside him was the size of a church door and stained dark, with the blood of things that had not been men.

He did not look like a monster. He looked like a man who had been *eaten* by monsters, and who had clawed his way back out of their bellies, again and again.

But the mark. The mark was unholy.

"I am a servant of the Lord," Anderson said quietly. "Iscariot. Section XIII. It's my duty to destroy every abomination that walks God's earth." He laid a hand on his coat, where the bayonets were. "I'll ask you once. Are you an abomination?"

The swordsman finally turned his head and looked at him. It was a long look, with one eye, and it was very tired.

"I've been asking myself that," he said, "for about three years."

Anderson felt something shift in his chest. He did not know if it was pity, or recognition.

He stood. "Then let us find out together, my son."

**Guts**

Of course.

Of course it was a priest.

He took the Dragonslayer and stood.

## The Fight

**Anderson**

He moved first. The Lord's grace moved through him, quickening, and the world slowed to a crawl. He saw the swordsman's heartbeat, the rise of his breath, the iron arm turning. He had all the time in the world.

He spent it praying.

*Lord, give me the strength to judge truly.*

The swordsman moved.

Anderson had fought Alucard. He had fought the No-Life King himself, who tore apart armies laughing. He had never seen anything come at him the way that sword came at him: as big as a door and as heavy as a cart, and it moved like a *whip*.

It went through his shoulder and out his hip. It took him in half before he had finished his prayer.

His upper body hit the sand. His lower body took a step and fell.

Then the sword came again, and again, and again.

**Guts**

He felt the armor close on him. The Berserker's skull settled over his face, the spikes went into his arms, and the Hound came up from the dark and laughed.

*A priest*, the Hound said. *Eat him.*

He cut the priest in half. He cut the halves into quarters.

Then he stopped, breathing hard, and looked down. Something was wrong.

The pieces were moving.

They were crawling across the sand to each other. Flesh met flesh and *knitted*, bone slid into bone with a wet grinding sound. The priest's face, lying on its side, was looking up at Guts.

It was laughing.

"*Is that all?*" the priest said. He was on his knees, then on his feet, whole, his coat in rags and his chest wet with his own blood. "*Is that all, abomination?*"

Two bayonets were in his hands, glowing with a light Guts had seen before: in Farnese's eyes when she prayed, in the Holy See's candles. It was a hard, bright, clean light.

It burned when it cut him.

**Anderson**

*Again.*

He'd been cut down again. Cut through the ribs, the skull and the spine. He'd felt his body come apart around his soul, felt the Lord's grace hold the pieces close, and he got up.

*Again.*

The swordsman came at him like a storm, like a war in one body. And every time, Anderson rose and put two more bayonets into him.

He didn't count anymore. He'd been cut down four times, perhaps five. The swordsman was slowing now, bleeding under the black armor, but he never stopped. Every time Anderson rose, the man was there, sword already moving.

Anderson began to laugh. He couldn't help it.

"*Yes!*" he roared, getting up for the sixth time with his ribs still knitting. "*Yes! Come at me! Come at me with all your hate! Throw it all at me, and I'll still be standing!*"

He meant it. He was not mocking the man. He felt joy, a hard bright joy, the joy he felt every time he faced something that would not break.

He hadn't felt it like this since Alucard.

**Guts**

He'd killed the priest seven times now. Eight.

The man kept getting up.

Guts's body was screaming. The armor was eating him, the way it always did: bones snapping and setting crooked, muscles tearing and pulling taut again. The blessed blades had cut him in forty places, and every cut burned like a coal.

He dropped.

He felt himself drop, the black closing over him, and the armor dragged him up again. The Hound was howling. The Rage pulled him up out of the dark by the scruff of his neck.

He swung, and the priest came apart again.

The priest got up again.

He dropped, and the armor dragged him up. He swung. He dropped again, and this time the armor held him up and let him fall forward onto his knees. He couldn't stand. The dark was over his eyes like a hood.

*Rest*, something said. *You've done enough.*

It was her voice. Casca's.

*Not yet.*

His heart kicked in his chest like a mule. His eyes opened. He came up off the sand with the Dragonslayer, and it went through the priest from collarbone to hip. Then twice more, in pieces.

**Anderson**

Anderson came back together on the sand. His face was against the ground, and his hands were twitching as the nerves found each other again.

The swordsman was standing over him, breathing in great wet gasps.

*He came back*, Anderson thought. *He was dying, I saw it. And he came back.*

He had never seen a man do that. A ghoul, perhaps, or a vampire, or something stitched together by sorcery. Never a *man*. Never through nothing but his own will.

He rose.

He felt the Nail of Helena in its sheath against his heart. It was the relic that would make him something more than a man, for long enough to finish this. He'd sworn to use it on the No-Life King. He'd sworn to use it only when there was nothing left.

He looked at the swordsman and left it in its sheath.

He did not need it. The swordsman had nothing left either.

He struck, and the swordsman fell.

**Guts**

Down.

The armor pulled him up. Down.

Up. Down.

He swung once more and felt the priest come apart again. Then the armor let go of him, and he was lying on his back in the sand. He could feel his heart stuttering. One beat. A pause. One more.

*So this is how it goes*, he thought, and then: *no. Not like this. Not to a priest.*

But his hands wouldn't move.

The priest stood over him, whole again, with blood on his coat and blessed steel in both hands. The light was coming off him. It wasn't harsh now. It was soft, almost.

Guts waited for the blade.

**Anderson**

He stood over the swordsman with his bayonets raised.

The mark on the man's neck was bleeding. Anderson watched it bleed, and for the first time he really *looked* at it. It wasn't the mark of something that had made a pact with Hell. It was the mark of something that had been *sacrificed* to it. It was a brand, like the ones burned on cattle before the slaughter.

He had seen the look in the man's eye in the bar. It wasn't a monster's look. It was the look of a child at the orphanage door, the ones who came in from the street with nothing, who'd seen things no child should see. They looked at the world like it might bite them, and it often had.

The blessed blades hung in his hands.

*Lord*, he prayed, *what would You have me do?*

He knelt down in the sand.

"Lay on hands," Alexander Anderson murmured. He put his palms on the swordsman's chest, just above the stuttering heart. It was the oldest thing a paladin knew, older than the bayonets and the Nail and the Section. He kept it for the children at the orphanage. He had never once spent it on himself. "In nomine Patris, et Filii, et Spiritus Sancti."

The light came out of him, gold and warm and soft, and went into the swordsman.

The heart under his hands steadied. One beat, then two, then the slow rhythm of a body choosing, for now, to stay.

The mark on his neck stopped bleeding.

Anderson closed his eyes and gave thanks.

## After

Guts woke up at the bar.

Again. His cheek was against the wood, and the lamp was flickering. The bartender was polishing a glass.

He sat up slowly. Every muscle he had was screaming. He was bandaged from shoulder to hip, clean white linen wound carefully around his chest, his arm and the stump.

The priest was sitting two stools down, reading a small battered book.

Guts stared at him.

"Why?" he said.

The priest did not look up. He turned a page.

"I've spent my life hunting monsters," he said. "I know the smell of them. I know what they look like when they're dying, and what they look like when they're afraid. You're none of those things." He turned another page. "That mark was given to you. You didn't choose it. And a man who keeps standing back up with something like that on his neck isn't a monster, my son. He's a soldier."

Guts said nothing.

"Mind," the priest added, "I still think you're wrong about God."

Guts let out a breath that was almost a laugh. "Yeah. I figured."

"The Lord has broad shoulders," said the priest. "He can take your quarrel. I expect He'll be waiting when you're ready to stop having it." He smiled, and it was the smile Guts had seen when he first came through the door, the one that meant it. "I have a feeling that won't be for a while."

He tore a page out of his book, carefully, folded it once, and set it on the counter between them. Then he stood, put his coat on, and walked out the door.

Guts looked at the page for a long time.

He picked it up and unfolded it. One line was underlined in pencil.

*Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.*

He read it twice.

Then he crumpled it in his iron fist and set it down on the bar.

The bartender set a fresh cup in front of him. Guts drank it.

When he was finished, he picked up the crumpled page again, smoothed it flat against the wood as best he could, folded it, and tucked it inside his armor, next to his heart, where nobody would ever find it.

The Brand was quiet.

He got up, shouldered the Dragonslayer, and walked out into whatever world was waiting.
